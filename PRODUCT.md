# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Expert designers and frontend developers building production color systems and design tokens. They work iteratively: generate a calibrated palette, compare it with known references, tune channel curves, validate accessibility and gamut behavior, and export implementation-ready values.

## Product Purpose

Provide a technically rigorous environment for generating and refining Tailwind-calibrated color palettes. Success means users can understand why a palette looks the way it does, make controlled and reversible adjustments, compare alternatives, and export trustworthy tokens without hidden color transformations.

## Brand Personality

Technical, precise, analytical, and direct. The interface should feel like a professional color instrument: dense when useful, explicit about calculations, and quiet enough for the palette data to remain primary.

## Anti-references

Avoid playful consumer color pickers, decorative color effects, unexplained “magic” controls, hidden state, ambiguous transformations, and interfaces that rely on color alone to distinguish data. Avoid generic dashboard ornament and marketing-style presentation inside the working tool.

## Design Principles

1. Make the model legible: expose sources, transformations, constraints, and confidence rather than presenting unexplained results.
2. Preserve one canonical color state: alternate color spaces, overlays, previews, and exports are derived views.
3. Support progressive precision: make common adjustments fast while keeping advanced curve and per-shade control available.
4. Keep experimentation reversible: every transformation is previewable, undoable, resettable, and explicit about whether it preserves the seed anchor.
5. Distinguish data redundantly: use labels, line patterns, markers, and ordering in addition to color.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. All curve operations must have keyboard and numeric-input alternatives. Overlay references must remain distinguishable without color perception through line patterns, marker shapes, labels, and sufficient contrast. Respect reduced-motion preferences and never encode status or meaning through hue alone.
